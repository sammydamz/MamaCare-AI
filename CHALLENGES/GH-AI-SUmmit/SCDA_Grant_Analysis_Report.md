## 4. Cross-Cutting Analysis

### Core Problem the Grant Aims to Solve
Bridge the **digital gender divide** — not just providing access/devices but using digital tools to deliver measurable improvements in women's health, education, and economic resilience in low-resource African settings.

### How the Three Documents Connect
- **RFA** = strategic framework, rules, evaluation criteria
- **Application Form** = operational tool structured to directly evaluate the four 25-point criteria via specific essay prompts
- **Budget Guidelines** = financial parameters to prove cost-effectiveness and compliance

### Critical Requirements for Applicants
1. Credible pathway to **50,000+ women** in 18 months
2. Digital intervention linked to outcomes in **health, education, or agriculture**
3. Rigorous **safeguarding** against TF-GBV, fraud, backlash
4. **Gates Foundation Open Access Policy** compliance
5. Strong **partnership network** for delivery and sustainability

### Red Flags / Rejection Reasons
- "Tech for tech's sake" without sectoral impact
- Asking primarily for devices, infrastructure, or digital literacy curriculum
- For-profits using funds for core business/customer acquisition
- Ignoring women's lived realities (illiteracy, shared devices, social norms)
- AI-assisted proposal exceeding acceptable AI-text thresholds

### What a Strong Application Looks Like
- **Combines** Direct-to-Participant (D2P) digital platform + Last Mile Service Provider (LMSP) network (e.g., community health workers)
- Technology optimized for low-resource environments: **voice-based, low-bandwidth**
- Detailed TF-GBV mitigation strategies
- Clear, cost-effective sustainability plan beyond 18-month grant
- Evidence of prior implementation and operational capacity
